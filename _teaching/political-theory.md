overview: >-
  Political theory examines the concepts through which political life is
  understood, the assumptions that sustain those concepts, and the normative
  claims made about political order. Moving beyond the study of institutions,
  parties, elections, and current events, the course asks more fundamental
  questions: Why does the state exist? What distinguishes power from authority?
  What makes a political order legitimate? How is sovereignty constituted? Do
  law and justice necessarily point in the same direction? How should liberty,
  equality, and property be related? Is democracy reducible to majority rule?
  The course approaches political theory as the conceptual and normative ground
  of political inquiry, bringing political concepts, institutions, relations of
  power, and competing arguments into a common analytical frame. Its purpose is
  not to provide ready-made political answers, but to enable students to identify
  how political concepts are constructed, distinguish the assumptions on which
  arguments rest, compare theoretical traditions, and use these tools to
  evaluate contemporary political questions.

objectives:
  - "Explain the central concepts and arguments of political theory."
  - "Distinguish the structure and conditions of normative political arguments."
  - "Evaluate political approaches, texts, and speeches through conceptual analysis."
  - "Relate political concepts to one another across different theoretical traditions."
  - "Classify foundational texts and use them to interpret contemporary political questions."
