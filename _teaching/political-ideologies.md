overview: >-
  Political ideologies are systems of thought that interpret the political world
  and advance ideas about how political order should be established or changed.
  This course examines ideologies as historically situated formations that shape
  understandings of the individual, society, and power, and influence political
  practice. Liberalism, conservatism, socialism, anarchism, nationalism, fascism,
  feminism, ecologism, religious fundamentalism, and multiculturalism are considered through their
  central arguments and historical contexts. The course explores the relations
  between the individual and community, liberty and equality, state and market,
  and authority and change. Students compare the assumptions and political
  proposals of different ideologies, analyze their interpretations of political
  concepts, and assess their relevance to contemporary developments.

objectives:
  - "Explain the historical contexts and development of modern political ideologies."
  - "Compare the central arguments of major political ideologies and distinguish their differences."
  - "Analyze ideological interpretations of concepts such as liberty, equality, justice, authority, and democracy."
  - "Evaluate contemporary political developments from different ideological perspectives."
