## About the Article

This article revisits debates on the ideological character of Kemalism by examining the classificatory dispute between solidarism and corporatism in Turkish political thought. It argues that Taha Parla’s influential reading constructs a teleological line from solidarism to fascism through successive operations of definition, classification, and conflation.

The article tests this framework through the case of Hüseyin Nail Kubalı, a Kemalist jurist shaped by the Durkheimian-Gökalpian tradition. Kubalı should not be treated as representative of Kemalism as a whole. His case instead shows how solidarist premises could be articulated in liberal-democratic and pluralist terms, challenging the idea that they necessarily lead toward corporatism or fascism.

## Contribution

- Reconstructs Parla’s interpretation through the operations of definition, classification, and conflation.
- Uses Kubalı as a critical case to question a necessary progression from solidarism to fascism.
- Shows how Kemalist solidarism could also support liberal-democratic and pluralist political possibilities.
